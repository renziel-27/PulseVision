PulseVision System Insights & Architecture

Comprehensive technical documentation, models, database ER, and rPPG pipeline

PulseVision is a non-contact physiological assessment prototype that measures heart rate from webcam video using remote photoplethysmography (rPPG). By detecting subtle blood volume pulse (BVP) color variations in facial tissue, it extracts physiological signals without requiring physical contact.

End-to-End Processing Architecture

1. INPUT
Webcam Video Stream
Standard RGB sensor at 30 fps (HD 720p/1080p).
↓
2. VISION
Face Detection & Forehead ROI Isolation
Facial landmark geometry isolates stable forehead and upper cheek skin while masking eyes, mouth, and hair.
↓
3. SIGNAL
Temporal Filtering & Signal Quality Gate (SQI)
Bandpass filtering (0.7–3.0 Hz / 42–180 BPM), SNR verification, motion artifact detection, and luminance stability checking.
↓
4. ENSEMBLE
Multi-Algorithm Consensus (Traditional + Neural)
POS, CHROM, GREEN, FastICA, and TS-CAN execute concurrently. Outlier rejection with concordance spread ≤ 12 BPM.
↓
5. OUTPUT
BPM Output, Database Persistence & Clinical Reporting
Results persisted to MySQL database; paired against smartwatch reference on Page 3; exported to PDF/CSV.

All valid implemented signal processing and deep-learning methods run simultaneously behind the scenes. PulseVision combines them via median consensus weighting to ensure high robustness against individual channel noise.

POS (Plane-Orthogonal-to-Skin) Active

Mathematical projection of normalized RGB signals onto an orthogonal plane to isolate hemoglobin absorption pulses from specular surface reflections.

CHROM (Chrominance-based rPPG) Active

Linear combination of chrominance difference signals (Xs and Ys) that cancels out standardized illumination fluctuations.

GREEN (Direct Green Absorption) Active

Tracks peak optical absorption of oxygenated hemoglobin (500–600 nm) across isolated facial pixels.

FastICA (Independent Component Analysis) Active

Decomposes RGB traces into statistically independent subcomponents, selecting the source with the maximum spectral peak sharpness in the physiological range.

TS-CAN 1D (Temporal Shift Convolutional Network) Active

End-to-end temporal shift neural model with attention modules operating on normalized facial RGB series for deep rPPG pulse extraction.

TS-CAN 2D Video Tensor Network Available (GPU Optional)

Full spatio-temporal 2D video convolutional neural network for frame-level tensor inference.

Validation compares contactless PulseVision estimates against consumer optical smartwatches worn on the wrist during identical measurement windows.

MAE (Mean Absolute Error)
MAE = (1/N) * Σ |PV_i - Ref_i|

Measures average error magnitude in beats per minute across valid trials.

RMSE (Root Mean Square Error)
RMSE = √[ (1/N) * Σ (PV_i - Ref_i)² ]

Penalizes larger outliers more heavily to assess prediction dispersion.

Pearson Correlation (r)
r = Cov(PV, Ref) / [σ_PV * σ_Ref]

Quantifies linear tracking agreement between camera and reference.

Bland–Altman 95% LoA
LoA = Mean Bias ± 1.96 * SD_diff

Identifies systematic over- or under-estimation bias across the measurement range.

🛡️ Academic & Research Prototype Disclaimer

PulseVision AI is an experimental computer-vision research platform. It is NOT a certified medical diagnostic device, has not been FDA-cleared or CE-marked, and should not be used as a substitute for professional clinical medical equipment or consultation.

Known Physical & Technical Limitations

  • Lighting Sensitivity: Low illumination (< 150 lux) or rapid ambient lighting flicker degrades RGB signal-to-noise ratio.
  • Motion Artifacts: Speaking, laughing, or excessive head movement introduces dominant motion frequencies that can obscure the cardiac signal.
  • Consumer Smartwatch Reference: Smartwatches are consumer-grade optical PPG devices and are not certified clinical ECG standards.
  • Physiological Range: Optical rPPG is benchmarked primarily for resting and recovery heart rates between 40 and 180 BPM.
